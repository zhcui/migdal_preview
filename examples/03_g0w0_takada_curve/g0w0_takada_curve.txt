Example 03: G0W0/Takada density-curve tutorial

Question:
  How does the leading pairing eigenvalue lambda(n) change when the
  gap kernel is truncated, kept untruncated, or projected following Takada?

Density scan:
  6 points from 1.000e+23 down to 1.000e+16 cm^-3

Methods:
  G0W0_trunc: sparse-IR G0W0 with Matsubara-frequency truncation.
  G0W0_no_trunc_cellavg: no-truncation sparse-IR G0W0 with automatic diagonal treatment.
  Takada_projection: dense projected solver in x = xi / eps_F.

Driver settings used:
  The sparse-IR G0W0 curves use q_interp_mode='q_singular' and
  diag_average='auto'. q_singular requires diagonal cell averages,
  so the truncated and no-truncation curves share that treatment.
  TakadaProjection uses its built-in grid defaults:
  n_q=20, n_theta=12, n_neg=48, n_pos=52, x_max=1e3.

Run settings:
  radial nq=500, k-left=200, k-right=200, k-shell=[50, 5000]
  q_interp_mode=q_singular, diag_average_mode=auto
  sparse-IR lambda=1e+09, ir_file=examples/ir.h5

Result snapshot:
  G0W0_trunc lambda range: -2.3905e-06 to 0.809056
  G0W0_no_trunc_cellavg lambda range: 0.725392 to 4.68245
  Takada_projection lambda range: 0.00140885 to 0.842883
